Why Can't We Just "Round" the Decimals?

When students first encounter continuous LP relaxation at (3.31, 1.88), the immediate instinct is to simply round to the nearest whole numbers. Let us test every possible integer rounding neighbor:

Testing All 4 Rounding Neighbors of (3.31, 1.88):

1. Round BOTH UP → (4, 2):
   4(4) + 2(2) = 20 ≤ 17  ✗ INFEASIBLE (Violates Constraint 2!)

2. Round x₁ UP, x₂ DOWN → (4, 1):
   4(4) + 2(1) = 18 ≤ 17  ✗ INFEASIBLE (Violates Constraint 2!)

3. Round BOTH DOWN → (3, 1):
   Feasible, but Z = 3(3) + 4(1) = 13  ! SUBOPTIMAL (Loses 4 units of profit!)

4. Round x₁ DOWN, x₂ UP → (3, 2):
   Feasible and Z = 3(3) + 4(2) = 17  ✓ THE OPTIMAL POINT!
⚠️
The Fundamental Lesson:
In 2 variables, there are 4 rounding candidates (2²). In a realistic model with 100 variables, there are 2¹⁰⁰ ≈ 1.26 × 10³⁰ combinations! Almost all of them violate constraints. Rounding is not an algorithm.
